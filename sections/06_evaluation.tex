\subsection{Evidence for S1 Today}
\label{sec:eval-promotion}
\label{sec:eval-m4}

Each cell repeats $n=30$ unless noted, reporting median and p95 over a single path.
Partitions cut only the targeted path with \textit{tc}, and impairment profiles follow
published mesh measurements~\cite{zimbelman2022mesh,kumbhar2015legacy}.

\subsubsection{Two-edge promotion under partition}
\label{sec:eval-two-edge}

Promotion is run across two independent vehicle edges, each with its own database, with
the incumbent partitioned from the network as in Figure~\ref{fig:promotion-timeline}. Two
outcomes were measured.

\emph{A superseded edge is told.} When A reconnects, the core refuses its batch with
HTTP~409 (\textit{not\_epoch\_holder}); A demotes itself, and its own accept path then
refuses writes. The core's journal is unchanged, and the rows A accepted while cut off
stay on A, unlabelled (\S~\ref{sec:model-gap}).

\emph{A holder that loses only its backhaul is told nothing.} B, the legitimate holder,
accepts decisions while its lease is valid (here $L=15$\,s), refuses them once the lease
lapses, and accepts again after the link returns and it renews. This is the lease's
cost: the fence cannot tell a superseded edge from a disconnected one, which is why the
model separates the lease from the grant.

We report no takeover latency, because the transition is dominated by $L$, a deployment
parameter rather than a property of the protocol.

\subsubsection{Fencing cost at one acceptor}

A promotion is driven end to end with every write attempt timed,
\promotionNPerPhase{} attempts per phase. Commits before and after the transition take a
median of \promotionMedianA{} and \promotionMedianC{}, and stale-epoch attempts are
refused in a median of \promotionMedianB{}, before any journal write. Across five runs
the fence holds exactly: 20/20 commits at epoch~1, 20/20 HTTP~409 rejections adding zero
journal rows, 20/20 commits at epoch~2, and a 200-row journal split 100/100 between
epochs with no \textit{event\_seq} value under both. Operator confirmation dominates
wall-clock time, so a promotion costs operator time, not network time.

\subsubsection{Model checking}

The strict TLA+ variant passes all six safety invariants over 38.56\,M distinct states
in 13.0\,min, whereas each weak variant is refuted in under a second:
\textit{SingleAuthority} in 185 states, \textit{EpochAuthorityCoupling} in 144, and
\textit{NoForkedJournal} in 1.0\,k. Both this model and the Hypothesis handoff model
cover handover from a reachable command edge and have no lease, so the cut-off case above
has a runtime witness only (\S~\ref{sec:non-claims}).

\subsection{Boundaries of the Design Space}
\label{sec:eval-scope}

\subsubsection{Raft leadership follows the reachable quorum}
\label{sec:eval-raft}

Each run hard-cuts the current leader $L_0$ of a three-voter
\textit{hashicorp/raft} cluster from the other two voters, after a sanity check
that its FSM assigns \textit{event\_seq} and enforces \textit{client\_event\_id}
idempotency. All twelve runs elect an $L_1 \ne L_0$ and commit five writes there
(median 3.08\,s, p95 4.43\,s). Isolated, $L_0$ stalls and fails with an apply
timeout during the election (median 269.9\,ms) and refuses immediately once it
has stepped down (0.4\,ms); reporting only the first would be cherry-picking its
worst phase. Both are correct Raft behavior~\cite{ongaro2014raft}, and both show why
consensus cannot carry the organization's decision: authority follows the reachable
quorum, and no input exists by which the organization can name the commander.

\subsubsection{CRDT merge cannot designate the author}
\label{sec:eval-crdt}

Four FastAPI replicas run Hanssen's operation-based Last-Writer-Wins (LWW) Element
Set~\cite{hanssen2025emergency} against constructed conflicting decisions
(Table~\ref{tab:crdt-comparison}). This tests merge as the authority mechanism, not
CRDTs as replication (\S~\ref{sec:positioning}), and it is a semantic falsification,
not a speed comparison. LWW discards the losing status from materialized state, whereas
\systemname{} keeps both writes in an attributed total order. Semantic rejection of
conflicting values is an additional guard that is not implemented
(\S~\ref{sec:non-claims}).

The baseline is deliberately lightweight and in-memory and converges only through
harness-triggered \textit{full\_sync()}. This favors CRDT, the correct bias for a
falsification: if merge cannot designate the author under favorable conditions, it
cannot under realistic ones.

\textit{Responder isolation.} One responder is isolated for ten seconds while
single-event POSTs go directly to \textit{/accept/event-batch}; they do not stall, and
stale or absent \textit{X-Command-Epoch} values are rejected before any journal work.

\textit{Headroom.} Saturation throughput at the sequencer is three orders of magnitude
above a generous demand of 10 decisions per minute, 0.17 events/s
(Table~\ref{tab:evaluation}). Fleets of 1, 3, 5 and 10 independent responder edges, each
with its own database and outbox, submitting simultaneously (five runs each, 20 events per
responder) delivered every event at every size; ten edges is where this single host runs
out of memory, not where the sequencer runs out of headroom. Throughput is therefore not
a concern for this workload.

\textit{Outbox.} Replaying 50 unique \textit{client\_event\_id} values five times each
leaves exactly 50 journal rows across $n=30$ runs, and 50 outbox submissions survive a
responder-Postgres power cycle, forwarding afterwards with no duplicates and consecutive
\textit{event\_seq}, journal-consistent about 2.6\,s after recovery. Through a schedule
of connectivity regimes (clear, mesh-stressed, mesh-degraded, isolated, clear) over
\scheduleRuns{} runs and \scheduleEvents{} events, the backlog rises only while the path to
the command edge is down, peaking at \schedulePeakBacklog{} events, and drains in
\scheduleDrainMedian{} (median) after restore; nothing is lost.

\textit{Backhaul.} A \textit{tc} cut of the command-to-core path recovers within about a
second of WAN heal for 1, 10 and 60~s outages (Table~\ref{tab:evaluation}), bounded by the
forward poll loop and backlog rather than by outage length.
