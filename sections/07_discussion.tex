\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The target deployment assumes an organizational CA with enrolled devices and users.
Revocation of a stolen tablet or vehicle node is eventual under total disconnection, and
a captured or malfunctioning command edge can poison the incident journal for as long as
it holds authority; a quorum-inferred design fails differently, not better, by moving
authority away from the designated commander. The architecture relies on operational
fencing and audit, not Byzantine tolerance; the artifact repository records the full
threat model.

\subsection{Alternatives That Move the Trade-off}
\label{sec:alternatives}

Three changes would move \systemname{} within the trade-off of \S~\ref{sec:tradeoff},
each at a stated cost.

\textit{Lease-wait promotion.} The core could withhold the next epoch until the old
holder's lease has lapsed, plus a margin for clock drift. No two edges would then accept
decisions at the same time, but every promotion around a cut-off edge would wait up to
$L$, and the core would have to track each holder's last renewal.

\textit{Late entries ordered by epoch.} Ordering the journal by (epoch,
\textit{event\_seq}) would let a superseded edge's entries join the journal when it
reconnects, ahead of every entry of the next epoch and without conflict. Readers would
then see history grow behind the tip, and the current commander should be alerted when
that happens.

\textit{Authority on the commander's credential.} Operational authority follows the
commander, not the vehicle. If authority were bound to a credential the commander
carries, such as a phone or smart card from the existing certificate infrastructure, a
commander who moves from A to B would carry authority, and even A's unforwarded entries,
across. That case would need neither a lease wait nor the core. The core would remain
the fallback for replacing a commander who cannot be reached.

\subsection{Evaluation Boundaries and Non-claims}
\label{sec:non-claims}

Both models cover coordinated handover only. The TLA+ model has one shared journal and
promotes only when the old command edge is reachable; the Hypothesis handoff model
promotes only after the old command edge has forwarded its whole journal; neither has a
lease. Model checking therefore establishes the six invariants for that case. The
cut-off case of Figure~\ref{fig:promotion-timeline} has a runtime witness only, and in it
\textit{DurabilityAcrossPromotion} does not hold for entries the old command edge never
forwarded.

The prototype has four further limits. The lease also stops a legitimate command edge
after $L$ without backhaul, and promotion needs the candidate to reach the core. Field
submissions pass the same epoch check and lease as decisions. \systemname{} records
decisions but does not yet deliver them to responder edges; any delivery acknowledgement
must not block, since a two- or three-phase commit would stall every decision while a
team is out of coverage (R1). Finally, authority binding is a protocol boundary, not
authentication: the emulation uses unauthenticated identifiers, so a node could assert
another's identity; promotion is authorized by access to the candidate vehicle, not by
the operator's credential; and semantic rejection of conflicting values is not
implemented.

The emulation isolates the authority, outbox, and fencing mechanisms from deployment
variables: physical mesh transport, security overlay cost, and Android client
integration. It does not claim physical Rajant mesh behavior, measured WireGuard or mTLS
overhead, or end-to-end Android validation, and the workload is single-incident and
limited-scale. Nor is the connectivity schedule a mobility model: no vehicle positions,
path loss, or RAN topology are simulated, and its literature-informed transitions are
synthetic, which makes them reproducible. Command-side writes are exercised through \textit{syncd}'s accept path
rather than the command \textit{ops-api}, and core bootstrap and backfill are synthetic
rather than measured.
