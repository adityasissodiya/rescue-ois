\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The target deployment assumes an organizational CA with enrolled devices and users;
the backend trusts certificate-derived identity only when the reverse proxy and service
boundary are correctly configured. Replayed field submissions are handled through
\textit{client\_event\_id} idempotency.

Several threats remain outside the prototype evaluation. Revocation of a stolen
tablet or vehicle node is eventual rather than immediate under total
disconnection, and because authority is designated rather than emergent, a
captured or malfunctioning command edge can poison the incident journal for as
long as it holds authority. That is the central trade-off of making command
authority explicit: a quorum-inferred design fails differently, not better, by
moving authority away from the designated commander. The architecture relies on operational fencing and audit rather than Byzantine
tolerance, and does not address a compromised CA, physical tamper resistance, or
audit-log integrity against a fully compromised node. These are deployment questions,
not properties established by the emulation; the artifact repository records the full
threat model.


\subsection{Evaluation Boundaries and Non-claims}
\label{sec:non-claims}

The evidence for each invariant matches the appropriate verification method.
\textit{SingleAuthority} and \textit{SingleCommand} quantify over all interleavings of
concurrent authority claims---properties model checking establishes exhaustively over
38.56\,M states but a service-path test can only sample. The service path now exercises
promotion across two independent edges (\S~\ref{sec:eval-two-edge}), so multi-edge
single authority has a runtime witness as well as a model one. Authority binding is a protocol boundary, not an authentication
one: the emulation uses unauthenticated identifiers, so a node could assert
another's identity, and semantic rejection of conflicting values remains
outside the prototype. \textit{EpochAuthorityCoupling},
\textit{NoForkedJournal}, \textit{DurabilityAcrossPromotion}, and
\textit{LocalIdempotency} have both model verification and live service-path
witnesses.

The emulation isolates the authority, outbox, and fencing mechanisms from deployment
variables---physical mesh transport, security overlay cost, and Android client
integration---that do not bear on the authority model's correctness. Accordingly, it
does not claim physical Rajant mesh behavior, measured WireGuard or mTLS overhead, or
end-to-end Android validation, and the workload is single-incident and limited-scale.
The safety properties are established by the TLA+ verification; the prototype shows the
protocol can be implemented and operated under partition, and the Hypothesis
state-machine tests check that its behavior agrees with the model on sampled traces.
