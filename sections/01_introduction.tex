\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Public-safety information systems must remain operational when commercial connectivity fails.
Rescue-service vehicles operate up to several hours away from regional dispatch centres. In that terrain, wireless connectivity alternates between full wide-area coverage, mesh-only operation, and total isolation~\cite{zimbelman2022mesh, wang2023overview}.
Disasters routinely degrade or destroy commercial telecom infrastructure precisely when
coordination demand is highest. Vehicles must continue acting on stale or partial
information, and field submissions entered on tablets must survive hours without
connectivity. In command-hierarchical environments the consequences of information loss
are wrong assignments, missed hazards, and conflicting orders.

The operational constraints are grounded in public Swedish rescue-service
guidance~\cite{rsg-rad201, rsg-kommunalplan}, which treats incident-planning information
as an ongoing, secrecy-restricted process rather than a static artifact; independent
studies of Swedish rescue-service information systems confirm these requirements and the
gaps in information flow~\cite{pilemalm2014enabling, westman2021mobilisation}.
From these public sources we derive three requirements and two deployment
constraints. The requirements are \textit{R1:} offline operation, \textit{R2:} durable forward-only
field submission, and \textit{R3:} attributable command authority. The deployment
constraints are avoiding cross-vehicle broadcast hazards and reusing existing
rescue-service certificate and identity infrastructure.

R3 is the demanding requirement. Every resource assignment, status transition, and
command hazard annotation---a \emph{decision}---must be attributable to the one
commander in charge when it was made, and all decisions must fall into one order. The
command post is a vehicle edge node, the \emph{command edge}, which is often cut off
from the regional core, and command can move to another vehicle
(Figure~\ref{fig:setting}). Meeting R3 involves
three properties that this paper keeps apart: \emph{authority}, which node may add a
decision; \emph{sequencing}, the order of accepted decisions; and \emph{replication},
how copies reach other nodes.

Existing designs choose the writer by other means. Consensus elects it from whichever
quorum is reachable~\cite{ongaro2014raft}, and a multi-writer conflict-free replicated
data type (CRDT) lets every replica write and lets its merge rule choose between
conflicting decisions~\cite{hanssen2025emergency}. In incident command, the command hierarchy
\emph{designates} the writer. Designation has a price under partition: while the command
edge is cut off, a design can give at most two of three things---the command edge keeps
recording, command moves to another vehicle without it, and one writer at a time
(\S~\ref{sec:tradeoff}).

We present \methodname{} (\systemname{}), a design discipline rather than a new
primitive: single-writer sequencing, transactional outboxes, idempotency keys, epochs,
and leases are all established~\cite{kleppmann2017ddia}. In database terms, the command
edge is the primary of a single-leader journal. What is specific is who chooses that
primary, where it runs, and how it moves: the command hierarchy designates it, it runs on
a vehicle that is often cut off, and only a fenced promotion moves it. Core-issued
epochs, a holder check at the core, and a renewable lease enforce the designation.
Outboxes and idempotent forwarding replicate, and any eventually consistent mechanism,
a CRDT included, could replace them. \systemname{} keeps recording for one lease window,
moves command as soon as the new vehicle reaches the core, and keeps the core's journal
single-writer; it does not stop two vehicles from accepting decisions during that
window. We make three contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item (C1) a problem analysis that separates authority, sequencing, and replication for incident command, states the trade-off a designated writer faces under partition, and draws the boundary between decisions and data that needs only weaker contracts;

  \item (C2) a realization on two independent vehicle edges---core-issued per-incident epochs, a holder check at the journal boundary, journal-prefix bootstrap, and a renewable authority lease---that states the role and the limit of each mechanism, including the window in which two vehicles can accept decisions;

  \item (C3) a partition-based evaluation on an executable prototype\footnote{The prototype repository is provided as an artifact with this submission.} (Docker Compose, \textit{tc-netem}) against Raft and a Hanssen-style last-writer-wins CRDT, extended to transitions between connectivity regimes and to fleets of up to ten responder edges, with TLA+~\cite{tlaplus} and Hypothesis~\cite{maciver2019hypothesis} models that check authority and sequencing for handover from a reachable commander.
\end{enumerate}
