In rescue-service incident response, one principal holds operational command authority at every moment, yet responders must keep working through responder-side and backhaul partitions, and command itself may move between vehicles as an incident unfolds. Standard distributed-systems tools do not fit: consensus moves the writer to whatever quorum is reachable, and conflict-free replicated data types (CRDTs) are mergeable-by-default, so neither lets an operator designate the authoritative writer under partition. We present Authority-Aligned Linearization (AAL), a design discipline composed from established mechanisms---single-leader replication, a durable outbox, idempotency keys, and epoch fencing---under a domain rule: the incident-journal linearization point sits at whichever vehicle-edge node currently holds externally assigned command authority. We claim no new consensus, replication, or fencing algorithm; the contribution is the authority/state-class boundary, its formalization, and its realization on a vehicle-edge architecture. We specify AAL in TLA+, mirror it with Hypothesis state machines, and implement it across two independent vehicle-edge instances with core-issued command epochs, journal-prefix bootstrap, and a renewable authority lease. Under a real partition, a promoted edge takes authority only after verifying it holds the complete journal prefix; a superseded edge that reconnects is refused at the journal boundary and demotes itself, while one that stays isolated cannot be told anything and instead fences itself when its lease lapses---an availability cost we report rather than hide. Against Hanssen-style CRDT-LWW and Raft baselines, CRDT merge cannot encode authority for non-commutative decisions, and Raft leadership follows the reachable quorum rather than the designated commander.
