In rescue-service incident response, the incident commander's decisions must form one attributable record. The command post is a vehicle that is often cut off from the regional core, and command can move to another vehicle. Meeting this requirement involves three properties, which we keep apart: authority (which node may add a decision), sequencing (the order of accepted decisions), and replication (how copies reach other nodes). Consensus chooses the writer by reachability, and a multi-writer conflict-free replicated data type (CRDT) lets its merge rule choose between conflicting decisions. Neither lets the command hierarchy designate the writer, although either could replicate a log that the designated writer has already ordered. While the command vehicle is cut off, a design can give at most two of three things: that vehicle keeps recording, command moves without it, and one writer at a time. We present Authority-Aligned Sequencing (\systemname{}), a design discipline built from established mechanisms. The command vehicle, designated by the command hierarchy, sequences decisions locally; core-issued epochs and a renewable lease enforce that designation; outboxes and idempotent forwarding replicate. \systemname{} keeps recording for one lease window, moves command as soon as the new vehicle reaches the core, and keeps the core's journal single-writer. During that window two vehicles can both accept decisions, and decisions that a superseded vehicle never forwarded are kept for review rather than merged. We implement \systemname{} on two independent vehicle edges under a real partition, model-check handover from a reachable commander in TLA+, and compare \systemname{} with Raft and a last-writer-wins CRDT.
