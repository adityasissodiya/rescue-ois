\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Many systems give one person at a time the authority to control devices at a distance. In
electric distribution, an operator in a control centre opens and closes breakers in
substations through a SCADA network. Links fail, so a device that must keep working
during an outage must keep obeying a holder of authority whom the other officers cannot
reach.

The same property helps an attacker. A device that obeys an isolated holder cannot tell a
link failure from an attack in which the adversary holds the holder's credentials and has
cut the other links. In the 2015 attack on three Ukrainian distribution companies, the
attackers stole credentials and issued commands through existing remote-access tools or
from a station similar to an operator console~\cite{eisac2016ukraine}. We use this attack
only as motivation. The report does not say how commands were authorized.

We study the following question.
\begin{quote}
\emph{When an old authority holder can still present valid credentials to disconnected
devices, under what conditions can authority expire and pass to a successor without
overlapping acceptance of commands from different holders, and what interruption of
legitimate service is unavoidable?}
\end{quote}

Standard tools answer parts of it. Byzantine replication gives a leader no authority
without a quorum~\cite{castro1999pbft}, so it cannot serve an isolated holder.
Intrusion-tolerant SCADA tolerates compromised servers but leaves a rogue operator out of
its threat model~\cite{babay2018spire}. Revocation lists need a message to reach the
device. Credentials that a quorum must keep renewing end without such a
message~\cite{luo2004ursa}, and leases give one holder at a time when grantors only
crash~\cite{vanrenesse2011nerio,gray1989leases}. The open part is how far these pieces go
when the holder and some officers are hostile, and what any protocol must concede.

\paragraph{What this seed contains}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Definitions} (\cref{sec:model}). Acceptance is a verifier event. Removal is
  measured from the instant withdrawals take effect. Isolation reach, which is
  existential, is separate from fresh-command availability, which is universal and
  conditional.
  \item \emph{A limit for every protocol} (\cref{sec:limit}). Isolation reach at a
  device implies equal hostile tenure in some run. This is the indistinguishability
  argument behind CAP-style results~\cite{gilbert2002cap}. We restate it for authority
  holders and do not claim the technique.
  \item \emph{Candidate rules and arguments} (\cref{sec:rules,sec:arguments}). \sys{}
  seed-v0 combines quorum-signed epoch grants with quorum-signed renewals that each device
  checks against its clock. Candidate arguments give separation $g - 2\eps$ between
  holders, no acceptance later than $H + 2\eps$ after withdrawal, and fresh-command
  availability for $H - \Delta - \delta - 4\eps$ after an outage starts, under stated
  assumptions.
  \item \emph{Executable evidence} (\cref{sec:evidence}). A kernel, a simulator with
  timestamped traces, a checker that shares no code with them, directed scenarios with
  negative controls, comparison mechanisms and a bounded TLA+ model.
  \item \emph{A positioning finding} (\cref{sec:memo}). Our Byzantine adaptation of
  exclusive quorum leases matches \sys{} in every directed comparison. We therefore
  claim no new mechanism \claim{C-NOVEL}.
\end{enumerate}
