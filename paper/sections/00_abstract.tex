Rescue-service edge systems must keep working during responder and backhaul partitions without letting concurrent replicas issue conflicting authority-bearing incident decisions. We present \methodname{} (AAL), the consistency discipline implemented by the \systemname{} prototype. AAL places the incident-journal linearization point at the edge node that currently holds operational command authority. Non-authority data (reference state, field submissions, materialized views) remains locally available and is forwarded eventually. Command transfer uses fenced promotion, not leader election. We contrast AAL with consensus-based state-machine replication, multi-primary replication, and conflict-free replicated data type (CRDT)-style merge. TLA+ and Hypothesis models check the single-authority and fenced-promotion invariants. A Docker Compose prototype measures field-submission propagation, wide-area network recovery, throughput, duplicate replay, and stale-epoch rejection on the service path. Partitions on either responder or backhaul links do not block command-edge progress, and stale-epoch traffic cannot poison the incident journal. The fence stale-rejection path is measured to be 2.5$\times$ cheaper than a successful commit, by construction. The cost is no automatic failover; promotion is operator-initiated.
