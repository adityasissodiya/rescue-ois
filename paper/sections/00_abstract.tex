Field devices that keep obeying an operator through a network outage cannot tell an
isolated loyal operator from an attacker who holds that operator's credentials and has cut
the links. We ask when command authority can expire and pass to a successor without
overlapping acceptance of commands from different holders, and what interruption of
legitimate service is unavoidable. The setting has $n$ officers, at most $f$ of them
compromised ($n \ge 3f + 1$), a holder who may be hostile, trusted verifiers at the devices
with clock error at most $\eps$, and a network that the adversary controls. This working
seed contributes three things. First, definitions that separate what some isolated run
lets a holder do (isolation reach) from what a loyal holder is guaranteed (fresh-command
availability), and that measure removal from the instant withdrawals take effect. With
them, a known indistinguishability argument shows that any isolation reach is also a
hostile tenure in some run. Second,
a candidate rule set, \sys{} seed-v0, with candidate arguments for exclusive succession,
for a withdrawal-to-expiry bound of $H + 2\eps$ and for conditional availability. No
person has reviewed these arguments. Third, executable evidence: a rule kernel, a
deterministic simulator, an independent trace checker, \SeedScenarioCount{} directed
scenarios with negative controls, and a small bounded TLA+ model. The evidence also
weakens the case for novelty. Removal by quorum renewal and expiry is known, and our
Byzantine adaptation of exclusive quorum leases behaves like \sys{} in every directed
comparison we ran. We state the open questions and the experiments that would settle them.
