We present an authority-aligned consistency discipline for rescue-service incident response: non-authority data is kept locally available and eventually forwarded, while authority-bearing incident decisions are linearized at the current operational command authority, with manual command transfer encoded as a fenced promotion rule. The design composes a regional core, vehicle edge nodes, durable outboxes, an append-only command-side journal, and bounded manual promotion: master data flows outward, responder submissions flow forward, and one command vehicle sequences authority-bearing incident events. The architecture chooses a different point in the design space from consensus-based state-machine replication, multi-primary replication, and CRDT-style merge. We evaluate a Docker Compose service-path prototype across bootstrap, field-edit propagation, WAN recovery, throughput, duplicate-replay, fenced-promotion service-path enforcement, and selected Raft comparison scenarios, complemented by TLA+/Hypothesis model-level evidence for the single-authority and promotion invariants. The results show the trade-off: responder and WAN partitions do not block command-local progress, and stale-epoch traffic is rejected at the current command and cannot poison the journal, but promotion is operator-initiated and does not provide automatic failover. Physical mesh deployment and Android validation are out of scope.
