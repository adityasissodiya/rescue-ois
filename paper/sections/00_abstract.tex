Swedish rescue services still rely heavily on paper binders for pre-incident plans, hazard information, and local operational knowledge, even though response work increasingly depends on digital maps, structured site data, and fast cross-unit information sharing. Existing digital approaches often assume persistent connectivity to a central backend, which breaks down at incident scenes where WAN access is unreliable or absent. This paper presents a three-tier offline-first architecture spanning a regional core, vehicle edge nodes, and field tablets, together with a tiered synchronization protocol designed for intermittently connected mesh-networked rescue fleets. The core design choice is a single-writer incident journal at the command vehicle combined with forward-only outbox replication from responder vehicles and tablets. The paper evaluates the approach in a controlled pilot deployment and reports bootstrap latency, field edit propagation, partition recovery, offline survivability, and promotion behavior under command-node loss. The contribution is an architectural pattern for maintaining a consistent shared incident picture without consensus protocols or multi-writer conflict resolution, plus an explicit account of the conditions under which that tradeoff is operationally appropriate.
