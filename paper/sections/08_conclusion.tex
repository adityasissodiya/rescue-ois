\section{Conclusion}

This paper presents an offline-first incident-information architecture for rescue-service operations over intermittently connected mesh networks. The central design move is a tiered synchronization model with one command-side incident writer, forward-only field edits, and durable local caches at every tier. The contribution is not only the architecture itself, but also a clear argument for why this constrained consistency model is preferable to multi-writer approaches in a safety-critical command hierarchy.

Three concrete lines of future work follow from the present contribution. First, formal protocol verification: the single-writer journal combined with manual promotion and forward-only outbox semantics is amenable to model checking, for example in TLA+, to verify the absence of split-brain commits across all interleavings of partition and promotion. Second, a physical pilot with at least two vehicles, three tablets, and a portable Rajant relay, instrumented along the observational lines of \cite{lindstrom2025tripod}, to replace the present emulation with field-realistic latency, mesh behaviour, and sustained survivability data. Third, a measurement campaign on the actual mesh hardware in subarctic forested terrain, to translate the qualitative connectivity findings of \cite{zimbelman2022mesh} into device-specific partition profiles for the synchronization protocol's evaluation.
