\section{Conclusion}

Authority-aligned linearization rests on an operational fact that consensus and
CRDT designs do not directly address: at every moment, one principal is
designated as authoritative. The incident-journal linearization point is the
command edge, the vehicle edge node that currently holds that authority.
Reference data, field submissions, materialized state, and audit forwarding sit
outside that boundary on weaker contracts. Command transfer uses fenced promotion
rather than leader election. Conventional offline-first synchronization does
not provide this. A backend-authoritative design cannot hold the command-decision
order under backhaul partition, the regime in which rescue-service incidents
operate.

The TLA+ model establishes the safety boundary, while the prototype demonstrates
its operational realization. Under strict fencing, the model verifies single
authority and no forked journal. The weak variant produces the expected
split-brain witness, and the prototype shows command-edge writes proceed
independently of responder reachability. The CRDT-LWW comparison shows the
trade-off: CRDT replicas accept local writes at multiple nodes and converge
quickly after anti-entropy exchange. AAL, by contrast, preserves one incident
journal and treats semantic conflict rejection as a model-level boundary
property. The cost is no automatic failover and lower write availability for
authority-bearing operations.

Future work is to deploy the prototype on a vehicle-mounted mesh, exercise fenced
promotion across multiple command edges, implement service-path semantic
rejection for authoritative conflicts, and add crash-boundary and Android
end-to-end tests. The harder comparison is empirical, not architectural: measure
the design against CoNICE-class consensus and CRDT-based emergency systems on
workloads where command authority is contested rather than designated.
