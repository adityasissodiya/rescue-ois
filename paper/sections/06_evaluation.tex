\section{Emulation \& Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
\label{sec:validation}

\subsection{Methodology and setup}
\label{sec:eval-env}

The evaluation follows the AAL contract. V-B tests the single-writer boundary
across command transitions and its cost. V-C tests ordinary authority-bearing
writes under responder isolation and characterises the serializer. V-D tests
the durable non-authority path under replay, restart, and mesh-grade link
impairment. V-E asks whether a CRDT-LWW baseline can preserve R3 on the same
workload shape. V-F measures support-path backhaul after WAN heal. V-B--V-D
exercise M1--M4 of \S\ref{sec:sync}; V-E--V-F delimit what \systemname{} does
not promise.

Measurements come from a single-host Docker Compose emulation: one regional
core, one command edge, and three responder-role edge nodes on a shared Docker
bridge. The propagation, recovery, and throughput cells are characterised
through a single responder path; the additional responders populate the
topology rather than the measured path, so the reported latencies are
per-path, not aggregate. The command-to-core WAN partition is injected
surgically with \textit{tc} on the command syncd: only traffic to and from the
core sync-api is dropped, so the command edge keeps reaching its local Postgres
and every responder throughout the outage---this is what lets
\S\ref{sec:eval-wan} measure backhaul recovery in isolation rather than a full
re-attach of the command node. Responder isolation, where required, detaches a
single responder edge from the bridge. The impairment cells use two
\textit{tc-netem} profiles informed by published mesh-network
measurements~\cite{zimbelman2022mesh,kumbhar2015legacy}: stressed
(80\,ms~$\pm$~30\,ms delay, 2\,\% loss) and degraded
(200\,ms~$\pm$~80\,ms delay, 8\,\% loss).

Parameter choices are fixed for single-host reproducibility rather than tuned.
Each cell repeats $n=30$ unless noted---small enough to keep the matrix runnable
on one host, large enough for stable median and p95 estimates, both reported.
Propagation queue depths of 1, 10, and 100 isolate per-event cost, exercise a
typical batch, and stress the polling/batching path. WAN partition durations of
1, 10, and 60\,s span short, medium, and long backhaul outages. Throughput uses
1000 concurrent submissions on two paths: direct \textit{/accept/event-batch}
posts saturate the journal serializer, and responder \textit{/api/events} posts
measure end-to-end M3 delivery. The idempotency cell replays 50 unique events
five times each. The TLA+ bounds (\S\ref{sec:eval-promotion}) use the largest
state spaces that complete in minutes on the same host.

\subsection{Fenced promotion rejects stale epochs cheaply}
\label{sec:eval-promotion}
\label{sec:eval-m4}

Fenced promotion is measured first because a failed fence can split authority.
The TLA+ and Hypothesis models establish bounded safety invariants; the
service-path run shows stale-epoch traffic is rejected before any
incident-journal work. Both commit and rejection paths add only single-digit
millisecond protocol cost, while operator confirmation remains a seconds-scale
wall-clock cost by design.

The protocol cost of fenced promotion (Figure~\ref{fig:promotion-cost}) is
single-digit milliseconds across all three phases of an active transition,
measured over $n=\promotionN$ events ($\promotionNPerPhase$ per phase). On
the commit paths, phase~A (pre-transition) has median \promotionMedianA{}
(p95 \promotionPNinetyFiveA{}), and phase~C (post-transition) has median
\promotionMedianC{} (p95 \promotionPNinetyFiveC{}). Phase~B, the
stale-rejection path, is $2.5\times$ cheaper than the commit paths (median
\promotionMedianB{}, p95 \promotionPNinetyFiveB{}) by construction: the
fence rejects stale traffic before it reaches the incident-journal write. The
saving comes from skipping the write for non-authoritative traffic, not from
adding a faster check on the write path. Operator confirmation sits in the
critical path by design, bounded below by deliberation under stress on the
order of seconds; the protocol contribution is two to four orders of
magnitude smaller than that floor. Promotion's wall-clock is therefore an
operator cost, not a network cost.

In five live service-path runs, phase~A commits 20/20 events at epoch~1,
phase~B returns 20/20 HTTP~409 rejections for the stale epoch and adds
zero incident-journal rows, and phase~C commits 20/20 events at epoch~2.
The incident journal contains 200 rows total, split 100/100 between epochs, with no
\textit{event\_seq} value appearing under both.

Table~\ref{tab:falsification-cost} contrasts the cost of verifying the strict
variant with the cost of falsifying the weak variant. The strict configuration ($V=3$, $J=7$,
$C=7$, $P=3$) verifies six safety invariants over 38.56\,M distinct states
in 13 minutes. Each weak variant fails in under a second of wall-clock;
counterexample traces have length 3, 3, and 5 for \textit{SingleAuthority},
\textit{EpochAuthorityCoupling}, and \textit{NoForkedJournal}, found after
exploring 185, 144, and 1{,}015 distinct states respectively. A Hypothesis
state-machine test mirrors this distinction in Python over 2000 examples
of length~50 with no strict violation and an on-demand weak counterexample.
Without the fence, single-authority fails immediately.

\subsection{Authority-bearing writes are independent of responder reachability}
\label{sec:eval-independence}
\label{sec:eval-m1}
\label{sec:eval-m2}

After V-B, the next question is whether ordinary authority-bearing writes
commit while a responder is unreachable; otherwise R3 would depend on
connectivity rather than the protocol. With one responder isolated for ten
seconds, 20 single-event POSTs to \textit{/accept/event-batch} commit 20/20
(HTTP~200), assign contiguous non-duplicate \textit{event\_seq} values, and
have median/p95 latency 5.8/7.3\,ms ($n=20$). The endpoint requires a current
\textit{X-Command-Epoch}; stale or absent epochs are rejected before journal
work. Responder reachability is not in the commit path.

Saturation throughput at the linearization point---direct concurrent POSTs to
\textit{/accept/event-batch}---is \TputDirectMedian{} events/s ($n=29$ after
warm-up exclusion; p95 \TputDirectPNinetyFive{}). This isolates the prototype
serializer: same-incident writes use \texttt{SELECT ... FOR UPDATE} over an
asyncpg pool capped at eight connections. End-to-end M3 throughput is reported
separately in \S\ref{sec:eval-m3}.

\subsection{Field submissions propagate under realistic intermittence}
\label{sec:eval-m3}

V-B and V-C cover authority-bearing writes. V-D covers M3: responder
submissions must reach the journal durably, without duplicate journal rows,
under retry, restart, and mesh-grade impairment.

Propagation latency is 710.1\,ms at queue depth 1 and 639.4\,ms at queue depth
10, then rises to 931.5\,ms at queue depth 100. The floor comes from the
responder push loop: \texttt{POLL\_INTERVAL\_S = 0.25}, with four-poll sleep
after empty results. The queue-depth-100 rise follows \texttt{BATCH\_SIZE =
50}: 100 events require two batches separated by the 250\,ms post-success
sleep, plus local submission and TCP setup; the measured delta over queue depth
10 is 292.1\,ms.

The duplicate-replay scenario issues 50 unique \textit{client\_event\_id}
values and replays each five times; across $n=30$ runs the journal contains
exactly 50 rows. Forwarding is at-least-once and deduplicated at the journal,
so submission is effectively exactly-once at the linearization point.

Durability across process restart is tested directly. Fifty submissions enter
the responder outbox, responder Postgres is power-cycled before forwarding, and
the stack recovers. All 50 survive, forward with no duplicates and contiguous
\textit{event\_seq}, and reach journal consistency about 2.6\,s after recovery.
This is the runtime witness for R2.

End-to-end submission throughput from responder ops-api to journal commit is
\TputEndToEndMedian{} events/s ($n=29$ after warm-up exclusion; p95
\TputEndToEndPNinetyFive{}). It includes durable outbox insert, push batching,
polling, and the M1/M2 commit path; it is distinct from the
\TputDirectMedian{} events/s linearization-point figure in
\S\ref{sec:eval-independence}.

Under the same \textit{tc-netem} profiles, each cell runs 20 measurements at
queue depths 1 and 10; all 80 measurements complete with no drops. Median/p95
latencies in ms are: stressed qd-1 1215/1494, stressed qd-10 2860/3355,
degraded qd-1 3647/5329, degraded qd-10 8104/10895. The heaviest tail is
degraded qd-10 (median 8.1\,s, p95 10.9\,s).

\subsection{CRDT semantics fail for authority-bearing data}
\label{sec:eval-crdt}

V-D shows the non-authority path holds under impairment and restart. V-E asks
the inverse: if the linearization boundary were removed and incident data were
mergeable by default, would R3 still hold?

The CRDT-LWW baseline is four FastAPI replicas using Hanssen's operation-based
Last-Writer-Wins Element Set with physical-clock timestamps~\cite{hanssen2025emergency}.
It preserves local acceptance but loses authority-bearing conflicts: concurrent
status updates converge to the latest timestamp, and a later update resurrects
an element deleted by another replica. The \systemname{} service path currently
orders and fences these events rather than rejecting them semantically; that gap
is a limitation in Section~\ref{sec:non-claims}.

The comparison is well-defined on field-edit propagation, no-partition write
acceptance, and the CRDT-LWW conflict cases. Infrastructure is asymmetric: the
CRDT baseline is in memory, has no background anti-entropy, and converges only
through harness-triggered \texttt{full\_sync()} calls, so the relabelled row is
post-quiescence forced sync, not live network impairment. AAL carries a durable
Postgres outbox. These asymmetries favor CRDT where it leads; they are retained
because the test is whether mergeable-by-default semantics can encode authority.

\subsection{Backhaul recovers within about a second of WAN heal}
\label{sec:eval-wan}

V-B through V-E cover paths with correctness choices. V-F covers support-path
backhaul: command-to-core forwarding after WAN heal. The partition is a
surgical \textit{tc} cut of the command-to-core path only; command Postgres and
responder reachability remain available, so the measurement isolates backhaul
flush time. Median recovery is 0.75, 0.91, and 0.23\,s for 1, 10, and 60\,s
partitions, with p95 about one second (0.92, 1.04, and 1.05\,s). Recovery is
bounded by the forward poll loop and backlog, not outage length.

Together, V-B shows the authority boundary holds across transitions; V-C shows
responder isolation cannot delay the journal; V-D shows field submissions cross
the boundary durably and idempotently; V-E shows mergeability does not encode
R3; and V-F shows support-path recovery is about one second. The cost remains
where \S\ref{sec:sync} places it: no automatic failover and lower write
availability for authority-bearing operations, concentrated at operator-driven
command transitions.
