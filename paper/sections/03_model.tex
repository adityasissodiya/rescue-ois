\section{Model and Properties}
\label{sec:model}

\Cref{sec:model,sec:rules} summarize the normative specification in the artifact
(\file{spec/protocol.md}).

\paragraph{Principals}
There are $n$ \emph{officers}. An officer is a person, and a \emph{signing agent} holds
the officer's key and applies the officer's decisions. The \emph{holder} is the officer
whose commands the devices currently obey. A \emph{verifier} at each device decides which
commands the device executes. Consent is a human decision. Renewal is automated: while an
officer consents to the holder, the agent signs renewals that its rules allow. A
\emph{withdrawal} takes effect when the agent records that it will sign no further renewal
for the current term.

\paragraph{Faults}
At most $f$ officers are \emph{compromised}: from some instant on, the adversary holds
their keys and signs anything with them. The holder may be one of them. All other agents
are \emph{honest}, and an honest agent that loses its durable state counts as
compromised. Verifiers are trusted: the adversary cannot change their code, state or
clock beyond the bound below, and a hostile holder cannot bypass them. The adversary
controls every link. It can delay without bound, drop, reorder, replay and inject any
message that it can construct, but it cannot forge an honest signature.

\paragraph{Time}
Real time $t$ is not observable. Each honest agent and verifier $p$ has a clock with
$|C_p(t) - t| \le \eps$, and verifier clocks never go backwards. $\eps$ must cover clock
holdover for the longest outage that a deployment accepts.

\begin{dfn}[Acceptance]
A verifier $v$ \emph{accepts} a command at real time $t$ when it durably appends the
command to its log and then releases it to the actuator. Acceptance is a verifier event.
It is not the completion of a physical action.
\end{dfn}

\begin{dfn}[Withdrawal instant]
\label{def:tw}
For a term (an epoch $k$ in \sys{}), $t_W(k)$ is the instant at which, for the first time,
$n - q + 1$ honest agents have each withdrawn from $k$ or moved past it. For mechanisms
without epochs, an agent withdraws by dropping consent for the holder.
\end{dfn}

Detection of a hostile holder, the human decision and coordination among officers all
happen before $t_W$. We report them separately and do not bound them.

\begin{dfn}[Exclusive succession]
\label{def:order}
A mechanism has \emph{epoch order with separation} $\gamma$ if, for every acceptance of
term $k$ at real time $t$ and every acceptance of a later term $j > k$ at real time $t'$,
at any verifiers, $t' - t \ge \gamma$.
\end{dfn}

The draft of 11 November 2026 used a pairwise condition instead: acceptances of two
different holders are at least $\gamma$ apart. That condition allows a pattern
$A, B, A$ in which each pair is far apart, so it is strictly weaker. Our checker
reports both (\cref{sec:method}).

\begin{dfn}[Withdrawal-to-expiry bound]
\label{def:wte}
A mechanism has \emph{withdrawal-to-expiry bound} $B$ if no verifier accepts a command
of term $k$ at a real time $t > t_W(k) + B$, in every run.
\end{dfn}

\begin{dfn}[Fresh-command availability]
\label{def:avail}
Under assumptions $\mathcal{A}$, a mechanism has \emph{fresh-command availability} $L$ if
in every run that satisfies $\mathcal{A}$, an outage that starts at $t_0$ leaves the
verifier accepting every fresh command that the holder creates and delivers at a real
time in $[t_0, t_0 + L]$.
\end{dfn}

\begin{dfn}[Isolation reach and hostile tenure]
\label{def:reach}
A mechanism has \emph{isolation reach} $P$ at $v$ if \emph{some} run with these properties
exists. All officers are honest. From $t_0$, only the holder $c$ and $v$ exchange
messages. $v$ accepts a command that $c$ \emph{created} at or after $t_0 + P$. It has
\emph{hostile tenure} $\Theta$ at $v$ if some run exists in which the adversary obtains
$c$'s key and state at $t_0$, every other officer withdraws at $t_0$, and $v$ accepts a
command of $c$ created at or after $t_0 + \Theta$.
\end{dfn}

Isolation reach is existential, and availability is universal. The draft of 11 November
also counted a command that was created before $t_0$ and delivered late. That inflates
the reach, so we exclude such commands.

\paragraph{Outside the model}
The properties above do not cover the meaning of commands. A holder may issue wrong or
dangerous commands while authorized, and may send different commands to different
verifiers. A bound in time is not a bound on physical damage. Expiry ends a term; it
does not revoke a key, and a compromised officer stays compromised. Clock attacks beyond
$\eps$, compromised verifiers and changes of the officer set are also outside the model.
